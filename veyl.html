Creed

"We are sent." Not the saviour — hoping only to be among those chosen to answer a need

Meaning

Not hidden. Unseen on purpose.

The name is inspired by the veil — not because VEYL wishes to conceal itself, but because appearance should never determine how a person's actions are judged. A volunteer could be anyone standing near you.

  • A volunteer may be your neighbour
  • A volunteer may be your father
  • A volunteer may be your daughter
  • A volunteer may be your teacher
  • A volunteer may be a stranger

A Service, In Practice

No spectacle. No performance. Simply mercy.

One example of how a single act unfolds — from the moment it's noticed, to the moment the volunteer walks away.

Notice

A volunteer notices someone who appears distressed. They are not expected to know the person's full circumstances — only to respond to what they can reasonably see.

Provide

The volunteer purchases food using donor funds — never their own claim to charity, always the mission's.

Document

The act is continuously recorded. Not for performance — so it can later be verified by anyone who asks.

Offer

The food is offered respectfully. Dignity comes before documentation, not after it.

Ask

Consent is requested before anything is published. A person's image is never taken from them.

Leave

The volunteer leaves. No name given. No applause sought.

— that is the whole of it.

How Trust Is Built

We don't ask for trust. We earn it.

Anonymity

Humility, not secrecy

Volunteers normally stay unknown to the public. If one becomes known by circumstance, that isn't a failure — the only real rule is that recognition never becomes the reason for service.

Transparency

Evidence, not promises

Every act leaves enough evidence to be independently verified — recordings, receipts, timestamps, interviews, photographs. The exact evidence depends on the situation, but it always exists.

Consent

Dignity comes first

Recipients are always asked before anything is published. If consent is later withdrawn, identifying content is removed, dignity is respected, and accountability is preserved through other evidence.

Accountability

A record that outlives the moment

Every volunteer keeps a verified record — hours, actions, supporting evidence — so the good done is never just a claim.
